Also Available In Fresno

Programmable LED Message Center Signs

A lot of Fresno owners shopping for a lighted sign are really after the changeable kind — a programmable LED sign that shows something different tomorrow than it does today. That product goes by a pile of different names: an electronic message center (EMC), an LED message board, an outdoor LED display, a scrolling LED sign or a digital marquee sign. They all describe the same thing — a grid of LEDs you update yourself from a phone, tablet or computer.

The usual setup on a Fresno storefront or roadside pole is both signs together: the lighted cabinet up top carrying your business name permanently, and the message center below it carrying today’s special, price, hours or announcement. One says who you are. The other says what is happening right now.

Check this before you order. Many towns around Fresno regulate electronic message centers separately from static signs, and limits on brightness, hold time, dwell time and animation are common. Confirm the rules with your local township or zoning office first — the same as you would for any freestanding sign. Easy financing available; ask about it when you request your written quote.

Shipping & Delivery

How Your Kit Gets To Fresno

Ground shipping fits Fresno for a simple reason: the ground network is already everywhere out here. About 95% of our kits ship FedEx Ground instead of freight, so the boxes come off the same truck that already services your street, whether that is an industrial row off Golden State Boulevard or a small commercial building in Clovis. Freight terminals and appointment windows never enter the picture.

What shows up. Four long boxes for a standard cabinet. Six when a larger frame needs splice joints to keep the package dimensions inside ground limits. Each welded section is cut to your size with the LEDs already installed inside it, and the power supply and mounting hardware are packed in alongside the frame. Everything lands together, so there is no second shipment sitting somewhere while your install day comes and goes.

Delivery day. No dock, no liftgate, no pallet jack, no scheduled delivery window. A lot of Fresno businesses are one or two people deep on a weekday, and pulling somebody off the counter or out of the shop bay to receive freight is a real cost. These boxes get carried in by one person and stacked against a wall until you have a morning free.

Open the boxes the day they land. Look the sections over even if the cartons look perfect, and do it before install day rather than the morning of. Photograph anything crushed, torn or bent and keep the packaging exactly as it arrived — that is what a carrier claim is built on. Finding a problem the day a box shows up is a phone call; finding it with a crew standing on a ladder is a lost day.

Where to put it. Aluminum handles Valley summers without complaint, and it does not rust in the winter damp either. The electronics need more care. Fresno swings from triple digits in July to weeks of tule fog and standing moisture in December, so keep the LEDs and the power supply in a dry indoor space from the day the boxes arrive until the day the cabinet goes up.

The exceptions go by truck. A cabinet too long to splice down into shippable sections, a pole or pylon project with structural parts that will not box, or several cabinets ordered at once are all cheaper and safer palletized — and if yours is one of those, it is priced that way in your written quote up front, not added later.
Before You Order

Measure Twice. Order Once.

Your kit is cut to a size, welded, and pre-lit before it ships, so the size has to be right the first time. In Fresno that means three phone calls before you order, not after.

1. Ask what you're allowed

If your address is inside city limits, start with the City of Fresno and ask for the group that handles sign permits and planning review. Outside the city, Fresno County is your authority. Clovis is a separate city with its own sign ordinance, and so are Sanger, Selma and Kingsburg — being three miles apart out here means nothing about sharing a rulebook. Ask for the maximum sign area and height for your zoning district, whether your sign counts as wall-mounted or freestanding, and what setback applies. Sign area is usually tied to your building frontage, so the answer is specific to your address — not to the block or to the business next door.

2. Ask your landlord

If you're in a plaza or center, the landlord's sign criteria sit on top of the city code and are often stricter. Cabinet depth, face color, letter height and mounting method are all commonly spelled out. Get the criteria sheet in writing before you pick a size — it is cheaper than rebuilding a cabinet that the center won't approve.

3. Ask about engineering and wiring

Plan for engineering on anything freestanding. California building and energy code, including Title 24, applies statewide, and this is seismic country, so footing depth and anchorage details generally have to come from an engineer licensed in California. Wind exposure on open Valley frontage can drive the design too. An illuminated sign typically needs its own electrical permit as well. Plan on a licensed electrician for the final hookup and inspection either way. We build the cabinet; the permit, the footing, the disconnect and the sign-off are local work done by local people.

Once you have your approved number, send it over and we cut to it exactly. Nothing here is legal or engineering advice — requirements change by jurisdiction and by site, so confirm yours with the City of Fresno or Fresno County, your landlord, and a licensed electrician before ordering.

Will a kit work on a pole sign out on a county road?

The cabinet side, yes — we weld it to your size and ship it in sections. The pole, the footing and the engineering are yours to arrange locally. Pole and pylon projects are also one of the cases where freight makes more sense than ground, so tell us it is a pole job when you request your quote.

Can I get a kit sized for a monument sign face?

Yes. Give us the opening dimensions you need to fill and we weld to those numbers. The same frame supports single-sided or double-sided, which matters on a two-way street like Shaw. Faces and graphics are not part of the standard kit; those are handled separately on our DIY Graphics page.

What does Fresno require for a lighted business sign?

Assume a permit is required. The City of Fresno handles signs inside city limits and Fresno County handles addresses outside them, and an illuminated cabinet commonly needs a separate electrical permit on top of the sign permit. Height and area limits vary by zone and by corridor, so get the approved dimensions in writing first. We weld to that size exactly.

Who should do the electrical hookup?

Hire a licensed electrician for that last step. These kits are not UL listed, and the power connection is inspected work in California no matter how simple it looks. Assemble the frame, mount the cabinet, set your faces — then hand it off. Getting an electrician lined up early is worth doing, since the permit inspection and the hookup tend to land in the same week.

What is the difference between a lighted cabinet sign and an LED message center?

A lighted cabinet sign shows one fixed printed face and lights it from behind — it carries your business name permanently. An LED message center shows changeable messages you update whenever you want. Most businesses run both on the same structure: the cabinet on top for the name and logo, the message center below it for today's special, price or hours.

An illuminated cabinet sign — also called a light box sign — is an aluminum box with lighting inside and translucent faces: the classic lighted sign above a storefront.
